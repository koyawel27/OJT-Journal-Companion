## 2. Source of truth

[OJT Journal Companion Brand Guidelines](../../BRAND_GUIDELINES.md) controls this exploration. The official name, tagline, Work Journal + Growth Journey concept, 70/30 balance, primary and fallback symbol directions, palette families, system typography, anti-traits, and application boundaries remain locked.

## 3. Evaluation criteria

The concepts are evaluated for:

- Work Journal clarity;
- Growth Journey subtlety;
- the locked 70% Work Journal / 30% Growth Journey balance;
- distinctiveness;
- small-size legibility;
- one-color strength;
- Light adaptability;
- Dark adaptability;
- professional tone;
- avoidance of the locked anti-traits;
- implementation simplicity; and
- favicon potential.

Scores describe this exploration pass, not permanent design value. A high progress score does not outweigh journal recognition or the required 70/30 balance.

## 4. Concept A — Rising Rule

### Concept A intent

Make progress feel like a natural continuation of recording work. The journal page carries the main meaning; the last ruled line changes course only near its end.

### Concept A construction

- Rounded 64-unit page outline with a 3.5-unit stroke.
- Two conventional journal rules.
- One final rule that begins horizontally, then rises through a restrained curve.
- No arrowhead, badge, endpoint marker, fold, or secondary symbol.
- The 16px fragment uses one conventional rule, a 4.5-unit optical stroke, and a shorter rising rule.

### Concept A strengths

- Strongest direct expression of the approved primary direction.
- Journal and growth ideas are structurally joined rather than placed beside one another.
- Keeps the journal visually dominant and reads closest to the intended 70/30 balance.
- Simple geometry remains strong in one color and reverses cleanly.
- The restrained curve feels calmer than an arrow or stepped line.

### Concept A weaknesses

- The progress meaning is quieter than Concept C and may need explanation on first exposure.
- If the curve becomes steeper or gains more turns during refinement, it could drift toward document analytics.
- At 16px, the distinction between an ordinary journal rule and the rising rule is present but subtle.

### Concept A small-size result

The page remains recognizable at 16px. The simplified fragment retains one straight rule and one rising rule without internal crowding. The progress cue becomes clearer at 24px and is comfortably legible by 32px.

### Concept A category-confusion risks

Moderate-low. The main risk is a chart-line reading if the final rise is made more angular, steep, or prominent. The current version avoids chart bars, axes, data points, and arrowheads.

## 5. Concept B — Guided Path

### Concept B intent

Represent reflection as a continuous path moving through organized work entries. A page margin and staggered rules establish journal structure while the path suggests measured development.

### Concept B construction

- Rounded page outline with a left guide or margin.
- Three staggered entry rules.
- One continuous rising path that moves from the lower margin through the entry field.
- The 16px fragment reduces the entry detail but retains the margin and path.

### Concept B strengths

- Most distinct internal composition in the set.
- Suggests that progress develops through recorded entries rather than appearing as a separate reward.
- The page margin adds a recognizable journal convention.
- The path remains arrow-free and works in one color.

### Concept B weaknesses

- The internal line intersections create visual congestion.
- The path can read as a route, maze, workflow, or process diagram rather than reflection.
- The journal rules and progress path compete for attention, weakening the intended 70/30 hierarchy.
- It requires more geometry and more optical correction than the other concepts.

### Concept B small-size result

The page outline survives at 16–24px, but the internal path and entry rules merge first. At 32px the intended construction becomes clearer; 48px is the first size where the path reads comfortably.

### Concept B category-confusion risks

High. Route/navigation, maze, workflow, and document-process readings are all plausible. These risks are visible in the current construction rather than merely theoretical.

## 6. Concept C — Progress Milestone

### Concept C intent

Make accumulated progress more immediately understandable while keeping the same journal-first foundation as Concept A.

### Concept C construction

- Rounded page outline and two journal rules.
- One restrained rising rule.
- A small circular terminal milestone at the endpoint.
- The 16px fragment enlarges the milestone slightly so it does not disappear.

### Concept C strengths

- Clearest progress meaning in the set.
- Retains strong journal recognition and one-color performance.
- The endpoint remains visible at 24–32px.
- Adapts cleanly to Light and Dark treatments, with or without an accent.
- Professional and simple enough for later icon-family alignment.

### Concept C weaknesses

- The endpoint can read as a chart data point, status dot, or notification mark.
- The progress idea is more prominent than in Concept A and can approach a 65/35 balance.
- At 16px the dot merges optically with the line endpoint, reducing the distinction between milestone and rounded linecap.

### Concept C small-size result

The page remains recognizable at 16px and the terminal remains present, though it reads more as a strong endpoint than a separate milestone. It performs well from 24px upward and is especially clear at 32–48px.

### Concept C category-confusion risks

Moderate. The current form avoids a badge outline and location-pin shape, but the terminal dot still resembles a chart data point if viewed outside the journal context.

## 7. Concept D — Page Corner Milestone

### Concept D intent

Provide the approved fallback when small-size behavior matters more than explicit journal ruling. A folded corner carries the page idea; one compact rising cue carries progress.

### Concept D construction

- Folded-page outline with a defined upper-right corner.
- No conventional journal rules.
- One short rising line with a small rounded-square terminal.
- The 16px fragment uses a 4.5-unit optical stroke and keeps the fold and terminal visible.

### Concept D strengths

- Strongest 16–24px silhouette in the set.
- Few elements, clean geometry, and excellent one-color behavior.
- Highest favicon potential in this exploration.
- The fold makes the page readable without relying on interior detail.
- Adapts cleanly to Light and Dark surfaces.

### Concept D weaknesses

- Reads as a generic file or document before it reads as a work journal.
- Removing ruled lines weakens the reflective-journal meaning.
- The endpoint can look like a page-status indicator.
- The folded corner adds a familiar document-icon convention rather than distinctive journal character.

### Concept D small-size result

The page, fold, and progress cue remain identifiable at 16px. It is the most stable compact silhouette, with no internal line collision at 24px or above.

### Concept D category-confusion risks

Moderate-high. Generic document, file-status, or attachment-adjacent readings remain plausible. The current version avoids a download arrow and paperclip shape.

## 9. Recommendation and selection outcome

**Leading concept: Concept A — Rising Rule.** It most directly follows the approved primary direction and holds the strongest journal-first 70/30 balance. Its restraint is an asset, provided Phase 5C-2 tests whether the progress cue is too quiet at 16–24px.

**Runner-up: Concept C — Progress Milestone.** It retains Concept A's strong journal structure and makes growth clearer. Refinement would need to reduce the chart-data-point risk without losing small-size visibility.

**Fallback: Concept D — Page Corner Milestone.** It is the strongest compact silhouette and best favicon candidate in this pass, but it sacrifices work-journal specificity for generic document clarity.

**Rejected direction: Concept B — Guided Path.** Its path is distinctive at larger sizes, but route/workflow/maze ambiguity and small-size congestion conflict with the calm, journal-first brief.

After reviewing the external Stitch Warm Journal package, the user selected that visual direction. It is treated as a polished production refinement of Concept A rather than a separate mark family. The decision and translation boundaries are recorded in [STITCH_DIRECTION_DECISION.md](STITCH_DIRECTION_DECISION.md).

## 10. Questions used for user review

1. Which concept feels most immediately like a personal work journal rather than a generic document?
2. Which progress cue best communicates quiet professional growth without feeling like analytics?
3. At 16–24px, is Concept A recognizable enough, or does Concept D's simpler silhouette feel safer?
4. Which concept feels least generic when shown beside the full OJT Journal Companion name?
5. Is Concept A's progress cue too weak, or is Concept C's endpoint already too corporate or data-like?

- renewed one-color, reverse, Light, and Dark checks;
